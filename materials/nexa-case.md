# Nexa - the running case bible

Every session applies its frameworks to Nexa. Numbers below are canon - all pages and
presenter notes must stay consistent with this file. Update here first if anything changes.

## The company

**Nexa** is a fictional B2B2C SaaS platform. Businesses subscribe to Nexa; their end
consumers use it. Nexa deliberately stays generic - it provides the digital layer
(storefront, booking, engagement, payments, analytics modules) that mid-size consumer
businesses run their customer experience on. Think "the platform behind the brands you
buy from" - no single vertical.

- Founded 9 years ago, Series C, 300 employees
- **$40M ARR**, growth 12% YoY (was 45% three years ago)
- **1,200 business clients** (the B), median client pays ~$33k/yr
- **8M end consumers** (the C) touch a Nexa-powered experience monthly
- Pricing: platform subscription + per-transaction take rate

## The situation (why the board is nervous)

- Net revenue retention **98% and falling** (was 118%)
- Logo churn **14%/yr**, concentrated in clients who never activated more than one module
- Two **AI-native competitors** launched in the last 18 months, priced 40% lower,
  onboarding in days instead of weeks
- Sales cycle lengthening; win rate down from 31% to 22%
- Cash: ~30 months runway; board wants a credible growth story before the next raise
- Internally: product team wants an AI copilot bet, sales wants enterprise push,
  CS wants activation fixes - no shared view of the real problem

## Session-by-session state of play

| Session | Nexa moment |
|---|---|
| 1 | Board asks "why is growth stalling?" - everyone answers with opinions. You structure the question (issue tree, MECE, pyramid). |
| 2 | Three competing bets on the table (AI copilot, enterprise push, activation fix). You run the decision (matrix, doors, pre-mortem). |
| 3 | Churn spike in Q2 cohort - find the real root cause (5 Whys, fishbone, is/is-not). Answer canon: churned clients were single-module, onboarded by a partner reseller with no activation playbook. |
| 4 | Two AI-native competitors force an external read (SWOT→TOWS, Five Forces, PESTEL). |
| 5 | Redesign the offer: current business model vs a lean-canvas test of a usage-priced "starter" tier (BMC, Lean Canvas, VPC). |
| 6 | Place Nexa on the business lifecycle: late growth hitting the "crisis of red tape"; plan Horizon 1/2/3 + Ansoff moves. |
| 7 | Map the customer lifecycle for BOTH sides (business clients AND end consumers), find the leaks, fix LTV/CAC math. |
| 8 | Capstone: assemble sessions 1-7 outputs into one board-ready strategy narrative. |

## Canon decisions (so later sessions don't contradict earlier ones)

- Session 2 verdict: activation fix is the reversible two-way door to start NOW;
  AI copilot enters as a Horizon 2 bet (confirmed in Session 6); enterprise push is
  deferred (one-way door, weakest matrix score).
- Session 3 root cause: partner-reseller onboarding gap → single-module clients →
  no habit → churn. Fix feeds Session 7's lifecycle work.
- Session 5 test: usage-priced starter tier validated on Lean Canvas as an experiment,
  not a committed pivot.
- Session 8 board narrative: "Fix the leak (H1 activation), fund the copilot (H2),
  explore the consumer-side network effect (H3)."
