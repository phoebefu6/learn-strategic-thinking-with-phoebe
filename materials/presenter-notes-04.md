# Presenter notes - Session 4 · Read the battlefield

45 minutes live. Audience includes the chairman and C-level - keep every framework tied to a
Nexa number and end every tool on an action, not a list.

## Preflight

- Whiteboard pre-drawn: SWOT 2x2 on the left, four empty TOWS cells on the right (labeled
  S×O offensive, W×O improve, S×T defend, W×T survive). Leave the arrows for the live moment.
- Second whiteboard or flipchart: blank Five Forces layout (rivalry center, entrants top,
  buyers right, suppliers left, substitutes bottom) with room for HIGH/MED/LOW stickers.
- Print or project the Nexa brief numbers: win rate 31% to 22%, churn 14% single-module,
  NRR 98% falling (multi-module 112%), rivals 40% cheaper onboarding in days, 8M consumers.
- Tables have pens and paper; one PESTEL letter card per table for the speed round.
- Page open in projector zoom mode; test the three SVG zooms (SWOT/TOWS, Five Forces, PESTEL).
- Have Session 3's root-cause artifact visible (partner onboarding gap) - Part 1 links to it.

## Never-cut beats

- The TOWS crossing arrows drawn live on the whiteboard - the physical crossing IS the lesson.
- "Lists describe, pairs decide" - the sentence the room should quote back.
- The Five Forces so-what: pressure source points at the moat. Do not leave forces as scores.
- Evidence rule in Apply-along 2: no fact from the brief, no score.
- The two moat candidates named out loud (multi-module switching costs, consumer-side
  network effect) - Sessions 5 and 6 both build on them.
- PESTEL trimmed to 2-3 starred forces with owners - the watchlist discipline is the takeaway.

## Cuts if long

- Part 3 live time: compress PESTEL to 2 minutes - show the diagram, star T and L, skip the
  lane-by-lane walk (the page carries it).
- Apply-along 3 becomes a 90-second show of hands ("which lane wins?") instead of table work.
- The honest-SWOT rules card: name the three rules in one breath, point to the page for depth.
- The second example callout in Part 2 (moat vs puddle) - tell it only if the room is ahead.
- Never cut apply-along 1 or 2; shave Q&A to 2 minutes before touching them.

## Q&A seeds

**Q1 (likely from the chairman): "We have done SWOTs for years. Why should this one change anything?"**
A: Because this one is not allowed to end as lists. Every quadrant pair must produce a play,
and every entry needs a fact - relative strengths, external threats. The test of today's SWOT
is that two of its plays (monetize consumer data, fix onboarding speed) show up as funded
moves in the Session 8 board narrative. If they do not, the SWOT failed, not the format.

**Q2: "The rivals are 40% cheaper. Why are we building moats instead of cutting price?"**
A: The forces read shows the pressure comes from low switching costs, not high price alone.
A price cut matches them for one quarter and donates margin forever - and they can go lower,
they were built cheaper. Switching costs and the consumer-data asset are structures they
cannot copy at any price this year. Session 5 does design a cheaper ENTRY point - but as a
tested experiment, not an across-the-board cut.

**Q3: "PESTEL feels academic. Do we really need the macro scan?"**
A: Two of the six lanes are already shaping this room's decisions: model-cost collapse is why
two rivals exist at all, and data/AI regulation governs the exact asset we just nominated as
our moat. The scan takes ten minutes a quarter and needs two named owners. The alternative is
finding out about the next force from a competitor's launch or a regulator's letter.
