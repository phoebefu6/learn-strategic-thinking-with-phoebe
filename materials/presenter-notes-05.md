## Never-cut beats

- The experiment-not-pivot framing, stated in the welcome AND at the kill-line step. This is
  canon and the chairman will remember whichever version he hears last.
- The two creaking blocks circled on the live BMC (channels, revenue) - Session 6 and the
  capstone both reference them.
- The four-block swap logic: a new bet has no partners, processes, or relationships - only
  assumptions. This is the whole BMC-vs-Lean decision rule.
- The riskiest assumption named verbatim: "self-serve clients will activate without human
  onboarding" - and why it is exactly Session 3's failure mode industrialized if false.
- The pre-committed kill line in Apply-along 2. Write a number on the board before moving on.
- "Fit is claimed left-to-right and verified with evidence" - the VPC sentence to quote back.

## Q&A seeds

**Q1 (likely from the chairman): "If the starter tier works, are we not just becoming a cheaper company?"**
A: No - the tier is a side door, not a new list price. The core subscription and take rate
stay untouched; the pilot tests whether smaller clients can self-activate and then climb into
the multi-module base, where NRR is 112%. If the pilot shows they activate and expand, it is
a growth engine. If they do not, the kill metric shuts it down after 50 clients - which is
the point of running it as a Lean Canvas experiment instead of a pricing decision.

**Q2: "Why test activation instead of demand? Surely the risk is whether anyone buys it."**
A: Demand is the comfortable assumption - a 40% price gap in the market already tells us
cheaper-and-faster sells. The fatal assumption is activation: Session 3 proved that clients
who onboard without a playbook stay single-module and churn. A starter tier that sells but
does not activate simply industrializes that failure mode at a lower price. So the kill
metric is activation to a second module in 30 days, not signups.

**Q3: "The consumer-data 'unfair advantage' - is that real or a slide?"**
A: Run it through VRIO, which is on the page. Valuable: plausibly, unproven in revenue. Rare
and inimitable: genuinely yes - 8M consumer touchpoints compound with time and the rivals
start from zero. Organized: honestly, no - nobody at Nexa monetizes it today. So it is a real
asset and not yet a moat; the O is an org-design action, and that is exactly how it should be
presented to the board rather than as a comfort blanket.
