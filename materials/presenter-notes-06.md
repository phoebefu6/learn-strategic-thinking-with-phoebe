- The Ansoff funding chain said out loud: penetration funds product development funds the diversification option. It is Session 8's narrative spine in miniature.

## Q&A seeds

**Q1 (likely from the chairman): "Why fund a copilot at all when the core is leaking? Fix the leak, bank the margin."**
A: Because the leak fix defends this S-curve and the copilot starts the next one - the AI-natives are not better at our curve, they started on a steeper one. H1 gets the majority of resources precisely so H2 stays affordable. The discipline is that the copilot is judged on trajectory with kill criteria, not funded on faith.

**Q2: "Isn't 70/20/10 arbitrary? Who says those are the right numbers?"**
A: Completely arbitrary - that is its job. It is a starting argument, not a law: it forces the allocation conversation to happen in explicit dollars someone can push against. The indefensible split is the one most companies actually run: 100/0/0, decided by nobody.

**Q3: "We put the consumer-side idea in H3. Doesn't 'option' just mean 'parked politely'?"**
A: No - parked means no money and no owner; an option means a small named budget buying answers to named learning questions by a named date. Ours: will consumers engage directly, and is the data loop real? If the answers come back negative, we kill it in public and cheaply. That is the opposite of parked - parked things never die, they just fade.
