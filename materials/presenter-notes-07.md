## Q&A seeds

**Q1 (likely from the CFO type): "Your LTV assumes churn stays constant for seven years. Nobody knows that."**
A: Correct - LTV is a planning estimate, not a forecast, and it is most honest as a COMPARISON: $62k vs $210k+ under identical assumptions. The gap is robust even if both absolute numbers drift. That is also why we compute per cohort and re-run quarterly rather than enshrining one blended number.

**Q2: "Why not fix the 22% win rate first? Acquisition is a bigger leak than activation."**
A: Because leaks compound downstream. Win more deals into a funnel that loses single-module clients at month six and you have bought expensive churn. Fix activation first and every future win is worth up to three times more - it literally raises the ROI of the acquisition fix we do next. Sequence, not either-or.

**Q3 (likely from the chairman): "8M consumers - why are we not just monetizing them directly, now?"**
A: Because today we cannot even measure them - no retention read, no RFM scores, no consumer NPS. Monetizing an uninstrumented audience risks the client relationships that pay the bills. The sequence: instrument the consumer lifecycle now (cheap - the payments logs already exist), prove the retention link to client churn, then the H3 option graduates on evidence. Session 6's learning-metric discipline, applied.
