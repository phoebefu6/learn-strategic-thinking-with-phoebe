# Presenter notes - Session 8 · The strategy stack (capstone)

Companion to `courses/08-the-strategy-stack.html`. This is the finale: selection + assembly + the pitch. Energy should feel like a graduation, not a lecture.

## Preflight

- **Participants must bring their artifacts** - the issue tree from Session 1 homework and everything they built on their own problem since. Send the reminder 48h and 2h before: "Bring your Session 1 issue tree and any later artifacts. You will pitch from them." No artifacts = Apply-along 2 collapses.
- **Pre-warn 3 pitch volunteers** for Apply-along 3. Recruit them a day ahead (ideally one senior voice - if the chairman or a C-level volunteers, the room's attention triples). Tell them the format: 90 seconds, standing, no slides, answer first.
- Have Session 8 page open with cards collapsed; test projector zoom.
- Print copies of `materials/framework-map.md` - one per seat. Physically handing it over is the closing move.
- Have Nexa's numbers in your head: $40M ARR, growth 45% to 12%, NRR 118% to 98%, churn 14% concentrated single-module, win rate 31% to 22%, ~30 months runway, $8M/yr NRR swing.
- Re-read the canon decisions in `materials/nexa-case.md` - the pitch you deliver in Part 3 must match them word for word.

## Never-cut beats

- **The triage drill (Apply-along 1).** It is the whole point of the capstone - selection under time pressure. If the room can route six cold questions, the course worked.
- **At least 2 live pitches (Apply-along 3).** Cut to 2 volunteers if time is tight, never to zero. Hearing a peer deliver answer-first in 90 seconds does more than any slide in the course.

## Cuts if running long

1. Chairman's five questions card - point to the table, take one row (why now), leave the rest as reading.
2. The 60-second triage card - the drill teaches it experientially anyway; name the four verbs and move.
3. Apply-along 2 neighbor swap - keep the solo kernel writing, drop the exchange.
4. Third pitch volunteer (protect two).
5. Never cut: the tree walkthrough, your from-memory pitch, the triage drill, two pitches, the framework-map handout.

## Q&A seeds (likely exec questions)

**Q: "Which three of the fifty should my team actually master?"**
A: Pick by your team's most common question type: a team that mostly diagnoses lives in is/is-not + 5 whys + pyramid; a team that mostly decides lives in doors + matrix + pre-mortem. The tree on today's page is exactly that prescription - and the pyramid is universal, everyone masters that one.

**Q: "Our real strategy process is political - the diagnosis is negotiated, not discovered. Does any of this survive contact?"**
A: Yes, and that is what the kernel is FOR: a written one-sentence diagnosis forces the negotiation into the open instead of hiding it in twelve initiatives. You may still lose the argument, but you will lose it explicitly - and the coherence test gives you neutral language ("what breaks if we cut this?") that challenges pet projects without challenging their owners.

**Q: "How is this different from hiring consultants to do it for us?"**
A: Same toolbox - that is not a secret, the sources are cited on every page. What consultants cannot rent you is the triage reflex in Monday's meeting, before any engagement is scoped. Use them for arms and legs on big analyses; keep the question-picking and the final pitch in this room. The board trusts a kernel more when the person delivering it owns the P&L.
